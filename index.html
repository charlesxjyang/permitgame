The Language of Investment

Net Present Value (NPV)

A dollar today is worth more than a dollar tomorrow. NPV takes all future cash flows from a project and discounts them back to today's value. If NPV > 0, the project creates value.

Internal Rate of Return (IRR)

The annual percentage return a project generates. Think of it like the "interest rate" your investment earns. Higher IRR = better investment.

The Hurdle Rate

Companies don't just need positive returns—they need returns that beat their hurdle rate.

The hurdle rate is the minimum acceptable return, typically 10-15% for energy projects. It accounts for:

  • Cost of capital (what investors expect)
  • Risk premium (uncertainty demands higher returns)
  • Opportunity cost (what else could this money do?)

If IRR > Hurdle Rate → Invest
If IRR < Hurdle Rate → Walk away

The Bottom Line
Permitting delays don't just postpone projects—they destroy value. Uncertainty compounds this effect. What started as a viable investment can become unbuildable, not because the underlying economics changed, but because regulatory friction consumed the margin. This is why streamlining permitting isn't just about speed—it's about unlocking projects that markets would otherwise fund.
